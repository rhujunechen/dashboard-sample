Data Story — What the Numbers Are Telling Us
📈
WBL Works — 86% of Graduates Land Jobs
Across three cohorts and 2,222 graduates, the Work-Based Learning programme consistently achieves above-80% employment. Kolej Komuniti leads at 92.3%, proving community-college vocational tracks have the highest market absorption.
▲ 86.3% avg employment rate
💹
Salaries Rising — RM 2,050 → RM 2,159 in 3 Years
Graduate earning power grew steadily, with ISM degree-holders commanding a premium at RM 2,659 vs. diploma-track averages near RM 1,940. The KK diploma cohort saw the fastest wage jump in 2025 (+12.4% YoY), signalling growing employer demand.
▲ +5.3% salary growth 2023–2025
🎯
Field-Match Gap: 3 in 10 Graduates Work Outside Their Discipline
While 70.8% of employed graduates work in their study field, nearly 30% are in mismatched roles. Combined with the low 28.7% employer retention rate — only 1 in 3 stays with their WBL placement company — curriculum-industry alignment needs closer attention.
⚠ 29.2% mismatch · 71.3% leave placement company
🏗️
Programme Scaling — Cohort Grew 14% in 3 Years
Intake expanded from 714 (2023) to 814 (2025), yet the 2025 overall employment rate dipped to 83.9%. Rapid scaling must be paired with employer-partnership expansion and MOA renewals targeting a placement retention rate above the current 35% goal.
→ Retention target: >35% (currently 28.7%)
"
The headline story is one of steady progress with a clear gap to close. WBL graduates are finding jobs — the 86.3% employment rate is a strong foundation. Salaries are climbing year-on-year, and the programme is growing. But the data flags two structural risks: field-match rates stagnating at ~70% and employer retention well below the 35% target. The 2025 employment dip (83.9%) as cohort size grew suggests the pipeline of partner employers has not kept pace with graduate supply. The strategic priority for 2026 is MOA renewal and deeper employer engagement — not only to hit retention targets, but to ensure salary growth and career-aligned placements are accessible to all graduates, not just ISM degree-holders.
